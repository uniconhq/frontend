# Words

The words Unicon uses on screen, one word per idea. Use these and no others;
when a page needs a word that is not here, add it here first, and check a
change against this list in review.

Nothing on screen names a file, a format key or how something is stored
(`task.yaml`, "the id", "declares", "pins", "form details", "mapping"): say what
the person is doing and what happens. A few places are the exception. "View
file" shows a contest's or a task's stored settings, read-only, for checking.
The workflow editor shows what a workflow's author must type: a port's name and a
reference such as `${{ inputs.language }}`. The formula editor shows a formula
as it is typed, `40 * st1 + 60 * st2`, so the names a formula reads (a value,
a group, a column, a fact such as `seconds_late`) are shown as they are
written. And where files are added to a task, the folders people make for it
are named as they make them: `public/` for a statement's pictures.

## Organiser leaderboards

- **Name.** The title people read. Its link and its name in other leaderboards’ formulas are shown below, fixed after the first save.
- **Who can see this leaderboard?** Organisers only (entrants never see it; use it to check the numbers first), Entrants (everyone entered in this contest, once signed in), or Anyone (people who are not signed in too, on the contest’s public page).
- **How many rows do they see?** Every row (every entrant’s row, ranked), Only their own row (each entrant sees their row and no place, like a grade), or The top rows. Rows tied with the last stay, and each entrant also sees their own row. **How many** sits beside that option.
- **Organisers always see every row.** With Organisers only, the row choice matters once entrants can see it. Someone who is not signed in has no row; choose Entrants for Only their own row.
- **Ranking**, **Rank by**, **then**, **Add a tie-break**. Each one after the first breaks the ties the ones before it leave. Accessible names identify each rank position and the order of its column.
- **Shown on the leaderboard**. Columns in their own shown order, with a number of decimals, or **shown as text**. Accessible names identify the column’s decimals and removal.
- **See it as**. The preview for one entrant.
- **Remove leaderboard**. Removes that leaderboard on save.

## The workflow graph

- **The workflow's page.** Its header names it `owner/name`, with a chip saying who can use it, Public standing out, and how many versions it has. The bar under it, which stays in view: **Showing** (The draft, or a version; its name is for a screen reader), whether the draft is saved (**All changes saved.**, or **Not saved yet: leaving this page loses these changes.**, as every editor says it), then Save, Publish version, Share and View YAML. Under the bar, once, a sentence on what is shown: **You are editing the draft. Publishing freezes it as saved, as a version tasks can choose.** On a version, Showing names it (**v2, the latest version** or **Version v1**), its bar has Share (for its owner) and View YAML, and its sentence is **You are looking at v2, a version. It never changes; tasks that chose it grade with it.** The banner: **Nothing to fix: this draft can be published as a version.**; **One problem to fix before publishing a version. You can still save the draft with it.** (or **3 problems … with them.**), each problem that has a box a link that opens it; or, when the check could not run, **The check did not run, so this page cannot say whether the draft can be published as a version:** and why. Under it, **Checking the latest change…** or **Checked as it stands now.**
- **The tray.** Over the graph at the top of a draft: "Click to add. The layout places it, and its drawer opens." Three groups:
  - **Inputs**: one button per type, named as the type is (Text, Number, True or false, One of a list, File, Folder), with what it is for under it. A new input is named after its type, with underscores as the platform's names have them: number, then number_2; a true or false input is named switch, and one of a list choice.
  - **Primitives**: the newest version of each, its title, what it does and its reference. "Unicon has no primitive this page can use." when there is none.
  - **Workflows**: **Find a workflow** filters by owner or name. Each run workflow the author can read, at each version, never the one being edited, says what it needs and gives, and "Private · v2". "Finding workflows…" while they are read, "The workflows could not be read." if that fails, then "No other workflow you can read runs as a step." or "No workflow matches."

- **A box, closed.** Its name, and a pill with its kind: **input**, **primitive**, **workflow**, or the number of outputs. Then what it is, its reference, and what it does.
- **needs … · gives …**. The line under a closed step's head, naming its ports: "needs source, language · gives program". Selecting the box opens it, with a row for each port.
- **nothing yet**. What an open box's port says when nothing feeds it.
- **v3 is out**. The line under a workflow box's head when a newer version of it is out.
- **Open ›**. The foot of a workflow box, drawn as a stack; it opens the box. Its accessible name is "Open" and the step's name.
- **Inside a box.** Open › on the box or Open in its drawer zooms into the well: the box's workflow, read only with every box open, between **What feeds it** on the left and **What it feeds** on the right, each a button that opens a workflow box or goes back up with the box selected. Over it, the version, **v2 is out** when there is one, and **Details**, the step's drawer at the level above: editable at the draft, otherwise **Read only. Change it in the workflow that holds it, then move that workflow's step to its new version.** Undo, Redo and the tray are off inside a box.
- **Inside the workflow.** The breadcrumb over a box's inside: the workflow's name, then each box's step, the current one marked; each part goes back to its level.
- **None yet.** The outputs box's line when the workflow names no outputs.
- **A wire dropped on a box.** A wire may be dropped on a closed box as well as on a port. It goes to the input named as its source, else the first input given nothing, and is then refused or drawn as a drop on that port would be; a refusal names the port, which the closed box hides: **args: This takes text, not a file.** When none fits: **Every input of check is given something already. Open it to choose which to change.**, or **check takes nothing.** While a wire is dragged, a closed box that would take it is outlined and one that would not is faded.
- **Lifting a wire.** A wire's end dragged off an open step's port, when the wire is the port's whole value, moves to wherever it is dropped; dropped on nothing, the port is left empty. The port's menu does the same with **Remove the wire** and **Wire from**.
- **The drawer.** The box selected opens in a drawer from the right.
  - **A step's drawer**: its Name; what it runs; for a workflow box, **v2 is out.** with **Move to it**, which moves the step to that version unless a wire or value would no longer fit, and **Open**, which shows inside it, as the box's Open › does. Each Remove, of a step, an input or an output, is red. Then **What it takes**: one Select per port, named by the port, offering only the sources that fit, **Nothing yet** when none is wired; a port that takes text, a number, true or false or one of a list has **Value of args** under it, for a value written in instead of a wire. Then **What it gives**, the Version, Details and Remove.
  - **An input's drawer**: its name, type and what the task's form says, then **Read by**, the ports that read it as `compile.language`, or **Nothing yet**.
  - **The outputs' drawer**: a row per output, its Name, **From** (the step output it reads), Title and, for a number, Unit; then **Add an output**.

## Runs card

- **Runs.** The tests a submission is run on. Each row is one run and what it is given. A submission runs once for every row, and Scoring adds up what the runs give.
- **Name (optional).** Shown to entrants and in results in place of the file name.
- **Add files.** Drop many files at once. Files with the same name make one row (1.in and 1.ans), and the ending picks the column. **Where these files go** previews the pairing; **Add these** applies it and **Cancel** leaves it.
- **The entrant gives this one.** A cell menu choice; the cell reads **The entrant gives it · up to 10 MB**, or its chosen limit. **Largest size** changes that row’s limit. **You give this one** returns it to the organiser.
- **Replace**, **Remove**, **Remove row 3**. Cell and row menu actions, named by the row and input. An empty cell says **Drop a file here or choose one.**
- **Run each row more than once, with different settings.** The second table’s disclosure. **Each run also gets one of these. Every row above runs once with every row here.** Choose its inputs on the Inputs card, under **Table**, labelled Table 1, Table 2, and so on. **axis** is a documentation term only.
- With no changing input: **A submission runs once, since nothing changes from run to run. To run it on several tests, set an input to 'One for each run' on the Inputs card.**
- File pairing problems say **No column matches** or **Two files fill the same cell**. Files are kept in the chooser until those problems are fixed.

## The whole result

- **Also shown to entrants.** A run’s reports already show with its group. These show on their own.
- **Not shown.** A part with no entrant display. Organisers and leaderboards can still read it.
- **On its own**, **Files to download**, **With its runs**. Result sections and when compiled is already visible with a run.
- **what the entrant gets.** Result’s description. The flow is a map: click a value for its card, or a group for its column.
- An output may be shown **on its own**, whether it compiled **with no run**, and a file **to download**. An empty shown folder stays **A folder, empty for now**.

## Task runs and groups

- **Groups.** Named sets of runs that a value can add up together, such as the small tests. **all** is every run.
- **Shown to entrants.** The row below group names; each group and all have their own control.
- **No runs.** A group without a matching run. **No files or values yet** names an empty, untitled row.
- **A tick picks that row in every run.** With two tables, their rows are crossed. Counts show the product, such as “2 × 3 = 6 runs”.
- **text.** The made-up example of a text output in the runs table; only Verdict alternates accepted and wrong_answer.

## Organiser formula cards

- **Reads nothing: it is always the same.** A formula with no named source.
- **Read only.** Fields can be read but cannot be changed here. Its information links to the organisers page: “Your role here doesn’t change this. See who can on Task organisers.” Use Contest organisers or Org organisers for those levels.
- **the new column.** The tile tray’s name for a column whose name is still empty.
- **column_3.** The initial name of an added column, numbered by its place; the person can rename it. Its tray uses its visible name, or “the new column” before it has one.
- **to fix.** A formula whose kind cannot yet be checked.

### Formula tray shapes and cards

- **Start from**, **For each run**, **For each submission**, **Many into one**, **Pick one run, then read from it**, **Pick one submission then read from it**, **Maths, tests and text**, **Run a program** put tiles in the order a formula is built.
- **empty** is a formula with no words yet; **to fix** is one with words that cannot be checked.
- **Takes**, **Gives**, **e.g.**, **optional**, **or**, **About** label a tile's full explanation, each form's inputs and output, and a checked example. **at least**, **at most**, **written out** explain an input's limits.
- Short shapes use **a number**, **one number**, **numbers**, **text**, **numbers/text**, **number/text**, **a group**, **one run**, **a task's results**, **task results**, **one submission**, **a number or a submission**, **a value's name**, **ranking directions**, **run weights**, **a missing value**, **a program folder**, **a program**, **submissions**, **number/submission**, **a number or text**, **a language**, **for each run**, **for each submission**. A group's count is **a group · 8 runs** (or **1 run**); a task's shape is **a task's results**. **…** means other forms have different shapes.
- **Total score**, **Runs passed**, **Fastest time**, **Best submission**, **Points**, **Penalty** name starters. Each button shows the formula it fills in. Examples say what the formula works out, using the page's names, without an invented result.

## How to write

- Full sentences or clear labels. No clipped "Empty: X" shorthand: say "Uses the workflow's default when left empty", or put the default in the placeholder.
- Say what happens: "Saving publishes this task", "Entrants will see this at once".
- Buttons are verbs on their object: "Open the contest", "Add a value", "Save".
- Say who may do something as "can": "Only an admin of the contest can change these", not "changes these".
- A refusal says what is wrong and what to do: "That code is not right. Check it with the organisers."
- Settings that cannot be read "cannot be read", and are fixed; nothing "does not read" or is "mended".
- Spell words out: "RUNTIME ERROR", not "RUNTIME ERR".
- Write a size one way everywhere: "4 B", "12 KB", "3.5 MB" (`lib/size.ts`).
- A choice of up to four options whose labels are sentences shows them all, as radio options, each with what it means or an example under it, and a greyed one with why under it. A dropdown is for a longer list of short words; one with long labels takes the whole row, and a greyed choice says why in its own label.
- A choice made on every row of a list, such as a leaderboard's sort, is a dropdown on its row, not radio options repeated on each row; a choice another row already made is not offered again.
- Parts of one block stand apart by the form's usual gap; nothing touches the part above it.
- An input's help goes behind an ⓘ beside its title. No field shows a key name beside it.
- Mark what waits for the save as a setting's file does: "New", "Replaced", "To be removed".
- Say one thing in the same words on every page that says it: what a save does reads the same as its question and as its answer, and a value or a cell reads the same on the result, the leaderboards and Marking ("–", "being checked").
- A link goes only where its reader can follow it; where the thing is gone or closed for them, say so in place of the link.
- A question to the person names what they act on and what happens, in their words: "Grade submission 2 again?", not the system's grading, attempt or publication.
- A message about what was just done, such as a refused wire, goes on the next action or after a few seconds; one that needs reading stays until it is dismissed.
